# Writing YouTube content (EasyHealthcare)

Main use: short health-education videos made by doctors and clinics on EasyHealthcare (for example "When should you see a dentist for gum bleeding?" or "Managing sugar levels during Diwali"). The same rules work for product and marketing videos about EasyHealthcare.

## Before writing, confirm
- Who is watching (patients, caregivers, other doctors, clinic owners), what language they speak (English, Hindi, Hinglish or a regional language), and how long the video is (Short ≤ 60 s or long-form 5–12 min).
- The single takeaway, the call to action (book an appointment, call the clinic, watch next), and who is on camera (doctor or animation).

## Script structure (long-form)
1. **Hook (0–15 s):** a question or relatable symptom. No "Hi guys, welcome back".
2. **Credibility (≤ 10 s):** the speaker's name, qualification and speciality.
3. **Body:** 3–5 sections, each with a short heading that doubles as a chapter title. Mark each section `[ON-SCREEN: …]` and `[B-ROLL: …]`.
4. **Red flags:** "See a doctor immediately if…". Every symptom video needs this section.
5. **CTA + disclaimer:** spoken *and* in the description.

Shorts: hook → one fact → one action, in about 130 words, with captions burned in.

## Titles
Give 5 options of ≤ 60 characters each. Put the keyword patients actually search for near the start ("gum bleeding", not "gingivitis" alone). No clickbait that promises a cure, and no fear-bait.

## Description template
```
<2 lines that answer the question; these are shown above "more">

⏱ Chapters
00:00 <hook>
...

👩‍⚕️ About: Dr <Name>, <Qualification>, <Speciality>, <Clinic>, <City>
📅 Book: <clinic booking link>

⚠️ This video is for general education only and is not a substitute for a consultation. Do not start, stop or change any medicine without your doctor's advice. In an emergency call 112.

#<tag1> #<tag2> #<tag3>
```
- Chapters: the first one must be `00:00`, with at least 3 chapters each ≥ 10 s long.
- Tags: 10–15, mixing broad and specific terms plus the local-language spelling.

## Thumbnails
Give 3 concepts. Each has ≤ 4 words of large text, one clear face or object, and high contrast. Never show a real patient, and never show graphic before/after images of wounds or surgery.

## Medical guardrails (non-negotiable)
- **No patient-identifiable information:** no names, faces, case photos, reports or voices unless documented consent exists. Never take content from EasyHealthcare patient records.
- **No guaranteed outcomes** ("cure diabetes in 30 days"), no drug brand promotion, no dosing instructions beyond "as prescribed".
- **India:** NMC (Registered Medical Practitioner Professional Conduct) rules limit doctors' self-promotion. Keep it educational: no testimonials or fee discounts, and no "best doctor in <city>" claims. The Drugs & Magic Remedies (Objectionable Advertisements) Act prohibits claims to cure the listed conditions. When unsure, phrase it as education and tell the user to check with their compliance advisor.
- **YouTube medical misinformation policy:** do not contradict WHO or local health authority guidance on vaccines, treatments or transmission.
- Cite sources (WHO, ICMR, the relevant society guidelines) in the description when stating statistics.
- Mark the script draft "for review by the presenting doctor"; Claude does not give medical sign-off.
